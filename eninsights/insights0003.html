How to Find Real Factories in China Without Relying on Platforms

Instead of starting with online marketplaces where trading companies dominate, begin with official business registration data. Every legitimate factory in China holds a business license that clearly states its business scope. You can request a copy of the license and check the Chinese wording: “manufacturing” (制造) means factory, while “trading” (贸易) or “wholesale” (批发) means middleman. This single document tells you the truth before you spend a dollar.

Another powerful method is to hire a local inspection company or a freelance sourcing agent in China to conduct a simple factory visit. For less than two hundred US dollars, they can go to the supplier’s address, take live photos of the production line, count the workers, and record the equipment. A real factory will welcome such visits; a trading company will hesitate or give excuses about security.

You can also trace your existing supply chain. If you are already buying from a trading company, ask them for the factory name and address for your quality records. Many traders will refuse, but some will provide it if you explain that you need to create a supplier approval file for your own compliance team. Once you have the factory name, you can verify it independently.

Finally, attend industry trade fairs in China at least once. The Canton Fair in Guangzhou and specialized machinery fairs in Shanghai or Beijing are where real factories exhibit. At the fair, you can walk up to a booth, touch the products, and ask direct questions about production capacity. The staff at a factory booth will usually be engineers or production managers who can answer technical details immediately. Trading company booths often have polished salespeople but weak technical answers.
By combining registration checks, paid local visits, supply chain tracing, and trade fairs, you can find real Chinese factories without ever opening a platform. The key is to shift from online search to offline verification.
